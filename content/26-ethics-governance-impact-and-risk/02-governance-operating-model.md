# Governance Operating Model

## Learning objectives

Turn high-level principles into ownership, lifecycle gates, inventories, policies, and evidence using the NIST AI RMF structure.

## Governance as an operating system

Governance defines who may make which decisions, with what evidence, at what point, and with what accountability. A useful operating model includes:

- an inventory of AI systems, models, datasets, vendors, tools, owners, and status;
- risk tiers that determine review depth;
- approved and prohibited uses;
- data, security, procurement, evaluation, release, monitoring, and retirement policies;
- named business, technical, security, privacy, legal, and affected-user roles;
- escalation, exception, incident, and appeal processes;
- retained evidence showing that controls operated.

A committee without operational gates is not governance. Neither is a checklist completed after deployment.

## NIST AI RMF functions

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework organizes work into four functions:

- **Govern:** establish culture, accountability, policy, roles, and ongoing oversight.
- **Map:** understand context, intended use, affected parties, dependencies, and impacts.
- **Measure:** assess performance, trustworthiness characteristics, uncertainty, and risk.
- **Manage:** prioritize, respond, monitor, communicate, and improve.

Govern is cross-cutting. Mapping before measurement avoids selecting convenient metrics that do not match real harm. Management includes accepting, avoiding, mitigating, transferring, or stopping a risk, with an accountable owner and review date.

## Lifecycle gates

Define minimum evidence for discovery, prototype, pilot, limited release, wider release, major change, and retirement. A major model, data, tool, permission, or use-case change should trigger re-review. Vendor updates are changes even when your own code did not change.

## Free local practice

Create a one-page governance charter for a small organization. Define the inventory fields, three risk tiers, approval authority, prohibited uses, exception expiry, incident escalation, and evidence required before a pilot. Map each rule to Govern, Map, Measure, or Manage.

## Completion criteria

- Every system and decision has a named owner.
- Risk tier changes the required evidence and authority.
- Exceptions expire and cannot silently become policy.
- Vendor and configuration changes trigger reassessment.
