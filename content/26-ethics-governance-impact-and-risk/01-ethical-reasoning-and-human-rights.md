# Ethical Reasoning and Human Rights

## Learning objectives

Identify affected people, distinguish principles from operational controls, and analyze an AI use case across its full lifecycle.

## Ethics is a decision process

An ethical principle such as fairness, privacy, human autonomy, or prevention of harm does not implement itself. It must change requirements, data collection, system boundaries, tests, user controls, deployment decisions, and accountability.

Start with people rather than model features. Identify direct users, people represented in data, people affected by outputs, workers asked to supervise the system, communities exposed to cumulative effects, and people who cannot access or contest the service. Benefits and harms may fall on different groups.

Analyze the whole lifecycle: problem selection, procurement, data creation, design, development, evaluation, deployment, monitoring, incident handling, retirement, and deletion. A harmless-looking model can still participate in a harmful process if its output is used for surveillance, exclusion, manipulation, or an unreviewable decision.

## Questions that expose tradeoffs

- Is AI necessary, or does it make an unjust process faster?
- Who defines success and who bears errors?
- Can a person decline, obtain a non-AI path, or appeal?
- Does the system change power, workload, or job quality?
- Are benefits accessible across language, disability, income, and digital literacy?
- What environmental and infrastructure costs are created?
- Which uses should be prohibited even if technically feasible?

Do not reduce fairness to one metric. Different definitions can conflict, and group-level parity does not prove an individual outcome is justified. State the normative choice, context, and limitations.

## Free local practice

Choose a proposed employee-screening, customer-support, education, or public-service agent. Build a stakeholder map and lifecycle impact table. For each impact, record who experiences it, severity, reversibility, evidence, uncertainty, and a prevention or mitigation option. Include the alternative of not building the system.

## Completion criteria

- People affected indirectly are included.
- Principles are translated into requirements or controls.
- The analysis covers deployment, retirement, and non-use alternatives.
- Uncertainty and conflicts between values remain visible.
