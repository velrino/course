# Stakeholders, Work Design, and Adoption

## Learning objectives

Design adoption with the people doing and experiencing the work, prevent hidden labor, and create meaningful feedback and control.

## Adoption is a system property

Low adoption may signal a poor fit, unsafe workflow, missing trust, inadequate access, or added workload. It is not automatically resistance to be overcome. Involve frontline workers and affected users before selecting the solution, compensate participation where appropriate, and preserve a channel where criticism does not threaten employment or service access.

Map how roles change. Automation can remove one task while adding exception handling, data cleanup, reviewing, incident response, or emotional labor. Estimate this hidden work and assign capacity, training, authority, and escalation paths. A reviewer who is accountable for an agent's decisions must have time and power to reject them.

## Stakeholder plan

Classify stakeholders by how they are affected, not merely by organizational influence. For each group, document needs, risks, decisions they should influence, communication method, feedback route, accessibility requirements, and owner.

Set expectations accurately. Explain capabilities, limitations, changing behavior, data use, common errors, and fallback. Avoid anthropomorphic claims that imply understanding or responsibility the system does not have.

## Feedback and control

Feedback must connect to action. Define how reports are triaged, how users learn the outcome, and how recurring problems change data, prompts, controls, or product scope. Protect reporters from retaliation and avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.

Track adoption alongside quality and workload. High usage can reflect coercion; low usage can reflect exclusion. Use interviews, observation, support cases, error data, and quantitative measures together.

## Free local practice

Create a stakeholder and work-design assessment for a support agent. Compare current and proposed tasks for users, frontline staff, managers, security, privacy, and support teams. Design onboarding, an accessible non-AI path, reviewer training, feedback triage, and a monthly workload review.

## Completion criteria

- Frontline and affected people influence requirements and evaluation.
- New review and exception labor is measured and resourced.
- Feedback has ownership and a visible resolution path.
- Adoption metrics cannot hide coercion, exclusion, or degraded quality.
