# Run a Sustainable Weekly Learning Loop

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- plan study around outcomes and energy rather than ambitious hour totals;
- maintain a small portfolio of evidence;
- review progress with objective signals; and
- adjust the learning process without abandoning the goal.

## Use a weekly control loop

A sustainable plan is a feedback system, not a promise made once. Use five stages:

1. **Select:** choose one primary outcome and one smaller maintenance outcome.
2. **Predict:** write what evidence should exist by the end of the week.
3. **Execute:** work in short sessions using orient, explain, retrieve, and apply.
4. **Measure:** attempt a closed-source performance task.
5. **Adjust:** change scope, practice type, prerequisites, or schedule based on evidence.

Keep time as a constraint, not the success metric. "Studied six hours" can coexist with no durable result. "Implemented and explained HMAC verification, then repeated it three days later" is evidence.

## Design three session types

Use the time and attention you actually have:

- **Deep session, 45-90 minutes:** learn a mechanism and build a small artifact.
- **Practice session, 20-40 minutes:** solve changed examples, debug, or extend the artifact.
- **Retrieval session, 5-15 minutes:** recall prior material and update weak questions.

On difficult weeks, preserve retrieval sessions and reduce project scope. Continuity is more valuable than a heroic restart cycle.

## Build an evidence portfolio

For each outcome, retain a compact record:

```text
Outcome
|- baseline attempt
|- working artifact or decision record
|- verification output
|- explanation from memory
|- correction history
`- next transfer task
```

Do not include secrets, personal data, copied proprietary material, or enormous generated logs. A useful portfolio is inspectable. A future reviewer should be able to see what you intended, what ran, how you checked it, and what remains uncertain.

## Conduct a weekly review

Answer these questions with evidence:

- What can I now do without the source?
- Which error repeated, and what prerequisite does it reveal?
- Which practice produced transfer to a changed example?
- Which item felt fluent but failed retrieval?
- What should be removed, delayed, or split next week?

Avoid explaining every failure as lack of discipline. The task may be too large, feedback may arrive too late, a prerequisite may be missing, or the environment may be unreliable. Adjust the system that produced the result.

## Completion criteria

- [ ] The plan names an observable primary outcome.
- [ ] Every week ends in an artifact plus a verification method.
- [ ] Retrieval occurs on at least three separate days.
- [ ] The plan contains a stop rule for unproductive struggle.
- [ ] The final assessment includes a changed transfer task.
